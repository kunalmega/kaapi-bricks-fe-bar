# AI Demo Roleplay Playbook: Summary

For AI-scored demo roleplays: 30 minutes, an AI business persona, an AI technical persona, and a rubric.
The judge reads a transcript, so say everything out loud, including what's on screen.

## Before the call
- Have **one value number** in one currency, plus a **downside case** (what it becomes if the weakest assumption only half works).
- Label every number **measured** or **assumed**. Know what's built **today** versus what the **pilot adds**.
- Have demo tabs open, files ready to upload, and a backup for each live step.

## Set-up questions
- **Product and use case:** the product, the customer's pain, one line on what it does, and the value number.
- **Industry:** the industry plus the segment.
- **Personas:** give each a **title, what they own, and the metric they're measured on**.

## Opening (2–3 minutes)
Agenda and timing → ask the business persona for **their one number** and say where you'll address it → a short story hook → a roadmap of the steps → one line per persona on what to watch for.

## Every segment: tell → show → tell
The pain today → what you'll show → show it, one thing at a time, without narrating clicks → **"Alex, this is your ___ number"** → one technical line → transition.
Keep jargon out of the business persona's part.

## Path to production (for the technical persona)
Cover all eight at the same depth, saying what exists today and what the pilot adds:
1. deploy from code
2. dev → staging → prod
3. automated tests
4. rollback
5. bad data and schema drift
6. security controls (row filters, column masks, secrets)
7. identity and network (SSO, SCIM, Private Link)
8. monitoring and alerts

## Objections
**Acknowledge → ask one clarifying question → answer with options and trade-offs → next step.**
- Never say "I hope", "I believe", "very easy", or "it's configurable" without giving options.
- Before giving any date, name the dependencies and say you'll confirm with the account team.

## Close (about 2 minutes)
1. Lead with the persona's own number.
2. Present the rest of the value as upside.
3. Give the downside case.
4. Make their metric part of the go/no-go.
5. Give one next step per person, each with a date.
6. Recap the roadmap and thank them.

If they push on an assumption, answer straight away with numbers. Never invent support for an assumption.

## Delivery
- Replace fillers with pauses or specific check-ins.
- Pause after key numbers, and say names and figures clearly.
- Plan about 20 minutes of demo and 6–8 minutes of questions; never cut the close.
